Dossier source-285: Ralph Waldo Emerson (1841)

  • Author: Ralph Waldo Emerson
  • Title / Edition: Essays: First Series (Boston: James Munroe and Company, 1841 / Wikisource transcription)
  • Locator: Essay IX: “The Over-Soul”, paragraph 3
  • Language: English
  • Translation: Original language, no translation
  • Retained Verbatim Passage: > “The Supreme Critic on the errors of the past and the present, and the only prophet of that which must be, is that great nature in which we rest, as the earth lies in the soft arms of the atmosphere; that Unity, that Over-soul, within which every man’s particular being is contained and made one with all other; that common heart, of which all sincere conversation is the worship, to which all right action is submission; that overpowering reality which confutes our tricks and talents, and constrains every one to pass for what he is, and to speak from his character, and not from his tongue, and which evermore tends to pass into our thought and hand, and become wisdom, and virtue, and power, and beauty.”
  • Relevance: Formulates the Over-Soul as an overpowering reality, common heart, and transcendent unity demanding sincere conversation as worship and right action as submission.
  • Limitations: Philosophical-idealist essay; asserts universal spiritual presence as metaphysical intuition rather than empirical fact.

Dossier source-293: Ralph Waldo Emerson (1841)

  • Author: Ralph Waldo Emerson
  • Title / Edition: Essays: First Series (Boston: James Munroe and Company, 1841 / Wikisource transcription)
  • Locator: Essay IX: “The Over-Soul”, paragraph 6
  • Language: English
  • Translation: Original language, no translation
  • Retained Verbatim Passage: > “All goes to show that the soul in man is not an organ, but animates and exercises all the organs; is not a function, like the power of memory, of calculation, of comparison, but uses these as hands and feet; is not a faculty, but a light; is not the intellect or the will, but the master of the intellect and the will; is the background of our being, in which they lie, — an immensity not possessed and that cannot be possessed. From within or from behind, a light shines through us upon things, and makes us aware that we are nothing, but the light is all.”
  • Relevance: Describes the Over-Soul as an unpossessable master-light and background of being that commands human obedience and strips private ego of ultimate autonomy.
  • Limitations: Transcendentalist philosophical essay emphasizing interior mystical perception and divine immanence rather than empirical psychology.

Dossier source-268: Herman Melville (1851)

  • Author: Herman Melville
  • Title / Edition: Moby-Dick; or, The Whale (1851 US edition / Wikisource transcription)
  • Locator: Chapter 36: “The Quarter-Deck”
  • Language: English
  • Translation: Original language, no translation
  • Retained Verbatim Passage: > “All visible objects, man, are but as pasteboard masks. But in each event—in the living act, the undoubted deed—there, some unknown but still reasoning thing puts forth the mouldings of its features from behind the unreasoning mask. If man will strike, strike through the mask! How can the prisoner reach outside except by thrusting through the wall? To me, the white whale is that wall, shoved near to me. Sometimes I think there’s naught beyond. But ’tis enough. He tasks me; he heaps me; I see in him outrageous strength, with an inscrutable malice sinewing it. That inscrutable thing is chiefly what I hate; and be the white whale agent, or be the white whale principal, I will wreak that hate upon him.”
  • Relevance: Ahab articulates his vision of the White Whale as an inscrutable malicious principal or agent behind visible nature.
  • Limitations: Literary fiction presenting dramatic monologue of Ahab; represents fictionalized metaphysics and creative mythmaking rather than historical testimony.

Dossier source-272: Herman Melville (1851)

  • Author: Herman Melville
  • Title / Edition: Moby-Dick; or, The Whale (1851 US edition / Wikisource transcription)
  • Locator: Chapter 42: “The Whiteness of The Whale”
  • Language: English
  • Translation: Original language, no translation
  • Retained Verbatim Passage: > “Though in many of its aspects this visible world seems formed in love, the invisible spheres were formed in fright. But not yet have we solved the incantation of this whiteness, and learned why it appeals with such power to the soul; and more strange and far more portentous—why, as we have seen, it is at once the most meaning symbol of spiritual things, nay, the very veil of the Christian’s Deity; and yet should be as it is, the intensifying agent in things the most appalling to mankind.”
  • Relevance: Articulates the cosmic ambivalence of the white spirit—at once the veil of the Deity and the intensifying horror of invisible spheres formed in fright.
  • Limitations: Literary narrative reflection by Ishmael; allegorical and aesthetic meditation rather than theological dogma.

Dossier source-294: Moroni / Joseph Smith (1830)

  • Author: Moroni (trans. Joseph Smith)
  • Title / Edition: The Book of Mormon (Palmyra, NY: E. B. Grandin, 1830 / The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints online edition)
  • Locator: Moroni 10:4–5
  • Language: English
  • Translation: English translation attributed by tradition to Joseph Smith from reformed Egyptian plates; presented here in published English scriptural text.
  • Retained Verbatim Passage: > “4 And when ye shall receive these things, I would exhort you that ye would ask God, the Eternal Father, in the name of Christ, if these things are not true ; and if ye shall ask with a sincere heart , with real intent , having faith in Christ, he will manifest the truth of it unto you, by the power of the Holy Ghost. 5 And by the power of the Holy Ghost ye may know the truth of all things.”
  • Relevance: Establishes Moroni’s epistemological command: the reader is directed to test prophetic transmission not through external historical proof, but through internal pneumatic confirmation by the Holy Ghost under conditions of sincere intent and faith.
  • Limitations: Scriptural text regarded within Mormonism as translated ancient record; regarded by academic/external analysis as 19th-century American religious revelation. Theological truth claims cannot be treated as secular historical fact.

Dossier source-295: Moroni / Joseph Smith (1830)

  • Author: Moroni (trans. Joseph Smith)
  • Title / Edition: The Book of Mormon (Palmyra, NY: E. B. Grandin, 1830 / The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints online edition)
  • Locator: Moroni 10:27
  • Language: English
  • Translation: English translation attributed by tradition to Joseph Smith from reformed Egyptian plates; presented here in published English scriptural text.
  • Retained Verbatim Passage: > “27 And I exhort you to remember these things; for the time speedily cometh that ye shall know that I lie not, for ye shall see me at the bar of God; and the Lord God will say unto you: Did I not declare my words unto you, which were written by this man, like as one crying from the dead, yea, even as one speaking out of the dust ?”
  • Relevance: Moroni defines himself as a voice speaking out of the dust and crying from the dead, demanding remembrance under the ultimate sanction of the final judgment bar.
  • Limitations: Eschatological farewell and testimony; reflects apocalyptic American restorationist framing of ancient record keepers speaking posthumously from the soil.
